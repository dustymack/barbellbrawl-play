FIGHTER LOOKS & GEAR

Throwaway prototype. How far can a stick figure go before you lose yourself in a crew? Build your fighter, then drop it into a crew of 7 at real fight size and try to find yourself. Every option is a named part on the skeleton, so the whole art style can change later without anyone losing their look.

0 · BUILD YOUR FIGHTER

Look data (what the app would save)

        
No pixels, no colors in hex, no art. Just names of parts. A new art style is a new renderer for the same names.

    

    

  


  
1 · THE CREW: 7 FIGHTERS, ONE TARGET

  
You plus 6 crewmates hitting a training dummy. Find me shuffles the crew, hides the marker, and times how long it takes you to tap your own fighter. Try it at Fight size with Random, then Lookalikes, then Clones, and switch the color rule between tries.

  

  

  

  


  
2 · WEAPON RACK

  
Each weapon adds moves, not numbers. The finish is cosmetic only: competition plate colors (red 55, blue 45, yellow 35, green 25 lb) make a natural color ladder lifters already know. Tap a weapon to equip it. Stat requirements are placeholders (B13).

  

  


  
3 · SUPPORT GEAR

  
A second slot. Each item changes one move and has a real downside, so it's a choice, not an upgrade. Tap to equip.

  


  
4 · WHAT TO LOOK FOR

  
    
    
  • Find yourself at Fight size. Random crews should take well under a second. If Lookalikes or Clones are slow with "Own colors", that's the case for giving each player a slot color in the fight.
    • 
    
  • Silhouette test. Everyone flat white: which parts still tell people apart? Hair, hats, capes, body shape and weapons change the outline. Faces, colors and marks don't.
    • 
    
  • Seven colors is a lot. Try Grayscale and Crowd size: some slot colors get close to each other. The floor ring and the marker over your own head carry most of the load.
    • 
    
  • Style-proofing. Switch "Art" in the crew bar: the same 7 looks drawn as chalk, clean ink, pixel or neon. Looks are saved as part names (see the look data), so a future art change is a renderer change, not a save migration.
    • 
    
  • Rules this prototype uses: clothes sit inside the chalk outline, and dyed hair, hats, capes and gear get a thin chalk rim, so dark colors never vanish on the dark arena. Dome hats hide spiky hair.
